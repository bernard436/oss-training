Language models

What we give the model, and what we never give it

Give

  • The text of an email or a LineLog note, after names, emails and phone numbers are removed
  • The event schema it must answer with
  • The product and line lists, to match names
  • The moves of a repair, to write the shift note

Never give

  • Any write right: plan, rules, SAP
  • The power to publish or to call a solver
  • SAP credentials or secrets
  • Personal data, or anything to rank people

The model proposes, code checks, a person decides. Every call goes through the model gateway (EU endpoint, structured answers only, logged), with a test set rerun at each change.

Governance

  • Weekly operating review, Friday, 30 min: Corinne, Sylvie, a shift leader, Oskar. Priorities, rules to change, blockers
  • Monthly steering committee, 60 min: Hervé, Julien, Nadia, Didier, Oskar. Milestones, go or stop, write-back

Adoption: decisions, not logins

  • Share of plans published from the tool
  • Overrides and their reasons
  • Events confirmed in the tool rather than by email
  • What-if questions asked by Stéphane
  • Night swaps chosen among the proposals

In one sentence

The solver does the combinatorics, the language model reads and writes text, the systems stay where they are, and every consequential decision stays with the person who is accountable for it today: Corinne publishes, Kevin swaps, Stéphane promises, Julien signs.
